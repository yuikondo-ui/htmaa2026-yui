      How these were inferred

      Every inference comes from rules run on this machine: keyword patterns over titles and channel names, and clock statistics over timestamps. No title was sent to a language model. English glosses of Japanese and Korean titles were written by hand for this mock. The confidence score grows with the number of matching views and is capped by hand for thin or indirect signals. Ad scores, futures and rising topics are the same kind of rule: counts, growth between periods, and life-stage templates.

      The export stores YouTube and TikTok times in UTC. The quietest six hours of each week decide the time zone: Japan time by default, UK time from late September to December 2021 and from late August 2023 to July 2024. Netflix only stores dates, so it has no clock and cannot place you.

      The body is the Acrylic Data Body model (Meshy, 150 mm print) reduced to 954 panels.

      Why four steps, not an off switch

      Monteiro et al. showed people inferences drawn from their own ChatGPT histories. Discomfort came less from sensitivity than from inferences that were wrong, or right but used in the wrong place. People wanted to correct wrong ones, keep accurate ones away from advertisers and other apps, and let short-lived ones expire, each next to the evidence behind it. The four steps here map those actions onto the brightness steps of the Acrylic Data Body.

      Switching one inference off does not make it unknowable. When others that point to the same thing stay lit, the panel says so. The assembled conclusions, ads and futures show the same problem one level up.

      Some sensitive inferences a platform could draw from this history are left out of the mock on purpose.

      Monteiro, K. et al. (2026). When Are LLM Inferences Acceptable? User Reactions and Control Preferences for Inferred Personal Information. arXiv:2605.10013
